Dishwasher issues are almost always traceable to a clogged drain pump, worn spray arm, failing water inlet valve, or a door latch that's no longer sealing correctly. Our technicians repair built-in and portable dishwashers from all major brands, including premium panel-ready models.

Standing water in the bottom after a cycle means the dishwasher [isn't draining](/problems/dishwasher-not-draining/) — a clogged drain pump or filter, a kinked or clogged drain hose, or a food-jammed check valve. It's often confused with a leak, but the fix is completely different, so we confirm which before quoting. A [dishwasher that's actually leaking onto the floor](/problems/dishwasher-leaking/) usually traces to the door gasket, a cracked sump, or the inlet valve.

In Las Vegas the number-one reason a dishwasher [stops cleaning well](/problems/dishwasher-not-cleaning-dishes/) is hard-water scale. It clogs the fine holes in the spray arms, coats the heating element so water never gets hot enough, and stiffens the wash-pump seals. We descale or replace the affected parts and check that the unit is getting hot enough inlet water in the first place. Cloudy glassware and gritty residue almost always come back to this.

A [dishwasher that won't start](/problems/dishwasher-wont-start/) is often just a door latch or switch that no longer tells the control it's closed — a quick fix — but can also be a failed control board or thermal fuse. Digital models showing an [error code](/error-codes/dishwasher/) get read on-site. We diagnose the exact cause before recommending a repair, so you're not guessing at parts.
